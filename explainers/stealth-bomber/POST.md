A 172-foot bomber can show up on radar about as small as a bird. Here's how 👇

🧪 Radar-absorbent coatings soak up part of the energy
🔥 Buried engines and top-side exhaust cut the heat signature
💣 Weapons stay in internal bays

It's NOT invisible. It just gets detected much later and much closer.

## Fact-check notes
- **172 ft (52.4 m) wingspan:** the published B-2 Spirit figure (U.S. Air Force fact sheet).
- **"About as small as a bird":** a widely repeated public comparison, not an official number. The video says "can look about as small as" and adds an on-screen footnote.
- **Flying wing, edge alignment, sawtooth trailing edge:** standard low-observable design principles (planform alignment). Edges parallel to each other concentrate reflections into a few narrow directions, away from the radar.
- **Radar-absorbent material:** converts part of the incident radar energy into heat. It reduces the echo but does not remove it.
- **Engines buried in the wing, top-mounted inlets and exhausts, exhaust mixed with cooler air:** documented B-2 features that cut infrared and radar signatures as seen from below.
- **Internal weapons bays:** external stores and pylons add strong reflectors.
- **"Not invisible":** stated explicitly. Stealth reduces and delays detection. Low-frequency radars, for example, can still detect such aircraft at some range.
- **10,000× smaller echo → ~10× shorter range:** from the radar range equation. Maximum detection range scales with the fourth root of radar cross-section, and ⁴√10,000 = 10.
- The aircraft model is an original, simplified procedural model. It shows the general layout, not exact dimensions or classified details.
